# LinkedIn Distribution Playbook

Status: Working hypotheses requiring continued validation

This file is deliberately separate from the voice corpus. It records observed distribution behavior and editorial sequencing. It must never cause the drafting system to distort Britt's voice merely to imitate an algorithm pattern.

## Current sequencing hypothesis

Britt recalls the effective sequence as approximately:

1. Personal story
2. A post about what Britt does, diagnoses, or sells
3. A learning, framework, or practical takeaway

This may describe a multi-post editorial rhythm rather than the internal structure of every post. Do not force all three elements into every piece. Validate it against the post history from February 2026 onward before turning it into a scheduling rule.

## Observed distribution behavior

The first hour has been a poor predictor of eventual reach. At least two recent posts initially appeared constrained and later expanded substantially outside Britt's network.

One tracked post was at approximately 154 impressions with roughly 90 percent in-network reach after 57 minutes, then later exceeded 10,000 impressions. A later observation recorded approximately 11,423 impressions with 68 percent out-of-network and 32 percent in-network distribution.

The useful operating rule is not “LinkedIn throttles Britt.” It is:

- Do not diagnose failure from the first hour alone.
- Capture early, 24-hour, 72-hour, and seven-day measurements where available.
- Track network mix alongside total impressions.
- Distinguish a slow initial test from a post that never escapes the existing network.
- Do not rewrite or delete a post merely because early distribution looks weak.

## Paid and organic interpretation

Thought Leader Ad authorization does not prove that paid distribution has begun. Britt's member analytics can blend organic and paid performance after sponsorship starts.

For the tracked LeanData post:

- 9,835 impressions was treated as the last guaranteed pre-authorization organic baseline.
- Growth to roughly 11,423 with a stable 68/32 out-of-network/in-network split was considered likely organic, but not provably organic without the advertiser's campaign report.
- The absence of a Sponsored label on Britt's own profile view does not prove that the ad is inactive.

Do not train an algorithm rule on blended paid and organic performance.

## What appears to work editorially

These are hypotheses to test against the larger February-to-present corpus:

- Personal stories perform when the detail is real and the professional meaning is earned rather than bolted on.
- Commercial posts work better when they demonstrate the diagnosis through a concrete operating problem instead of announcing an offer.
- Framework and learning posts work when they give the reader a usable distinction, diagnostic question, or operating implication.
- Posts can be long when the narrative or mechanism earns the length.
- Eccentric specificity, humor, and visible enthusiasm are assets, not noise to remove.
- A strong post does not always need a CTA.
- Offer routing should follow the subject of the post and the reader's likely problem, not a universal conversion requirement.

## Measurements needed

For posts from February 2026 onward, capture where available:

- publication date and time
- content mode
- opening move
- topic
- offer or CTA, if any
- impressions at comparable intervals
- reactions
- comments
- reposts
- saves
- profile views
- followers gained
- in-network and out-of-network percentage
- whether paid amplification occurred
- qualified conversations or pipeline influence

Do not optimize for impressions alone. Authority, qualified conversations, referrals, assessment starts, subscriptions, and commercial movement are separate outcomes.

## Decision boundary

Voice rules describe how Britt communicates. Distribution hypotheses describe what may affect reach. Commercial routing rules describe what action the content should support. Keep all three distinct, and require evidence before allowing a distribution hypothesis to override editorial judgment.
