I went through everything you sent me. I understand the company you’re trying to build, and I don’t think this is a generic hiring-assessment business. The larger idea is an independent evidence and trust layer for consequential workforce decisions. That’s the interesting part.

But I think we’ve been talking past each other a little. I came into this trying to help you identify a credible first commercial wedge and get market proof. You’re telling me the primary goal is to raise capital and build toward a strategic outcome.

I don’t think those are competing paths. If you want capital, you still need evidence that someone outside UNI believes in this enough to put something at risk. Customers, design partners, institutional partners, investors, a team. Research helps establish the category, but it doesn’t replace that proof.

I can’t tell you whether Microsoft or Workday will buy UNI, and I don’t think anybody can responsibly tell you that today. What we can do is get clear about what UNI would need to build for that question to make sense. What would they actually be buying? A team? Customers? Validation evidence? A credential network? A product people rely on? Something they can’t just build later?

I want us to leave with a real answer on the route you’re choosing, what it needs to prove, and what has to happen next. I’m not trying to make the company smaller. I’m trying to make the plan real.

2. What would make someone else believe it?

We need something outside UNI that shows this isn’t just a good argument in a room.

Ask him

Who’s on the team besides you, or who has to be formally committed for someone to believe this can be built?

What external proof exists today that isn’t your own research, product work, or conviction?

Who will put something at risk in the next 90 days: a customer, a design partner, an institutional collaborator, or an investor?

3. What does the first proof actually look like?

I don’t think early customers automatically turn UNI into a hiring-assessment company. It depends on what they buy and what it proves.

Ask him

What’s the first consequential workforce decision that demonstrates the bigger UNI + ETI idea?

What is the actual thing the customer receives, relies on, challenges, or carries forward?

How does that first customer prove this is an independent trust layer rather than another assessment product?

4. What would a buyer actually be buying?

We can’t predict whether Microsoft or Workday will buy UNI. We can get clear about what they’d need to see for the question to make sense.

Ask him

If Workday or Microsoft bought UNI in two years, what would they actually be buying?

Why would they buy it rather than build it or acquire it later for less?

Does selling to a platform undermine the independence that makes UNI valuable? If not, why not?
